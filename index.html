Marketing on one screen

Almost every marketing decision is about moving people between these five states — and knowing which step is actually leaking.

1 · Awareness They learn you exist. Measured in reach, impressions, share of search.
2 · Interest They engage. Measured in sessions, dwell time, follows, email sign-ups.
3 · Consideration They compare you. Measured in demos, trials, pricing-page views, carts.
4 · Conversion They buy. Measured in orders, closed-won, CAC, conversion rate.
5 · Retention They stay and refer. Measured in repeat rate, churn, NRR, referrals.
The single most useful habit

Write your funnel out with real numbers once a month. The biggest percentage drop between two consecutive steps is your constraint — and until you fix it, spending more at the top mostly buys a bigger leak. Most teams instinctively optimise step 1 because it is the easiest to buy.

Rules of thumb worth memorising

Benchmarks are context-dependent, but these hold up across enough companies to be a useful starting hypothesis. Treat each as a question to ask, not a law.

Common marketing rules of thumb with their origin and caveats
Rule of thumbWhere it comes fromWhen it breaks
LTV:CAC of 3:1 or better Venture-backed SaaS convention If your LTV horizon is 5 years and you are cash-constrained, a great ratio can still bankrupt you. Payback period matters more.
CAC payback under 12 months B2B SaaS benchmark Ecommerce usually needs payback on the first or second order. Enterprise with 120%+ NRR can justify 18–24 months.
Roughly 60% brand / 40% activation Les Binet & Peter Field, IPA databank analysis It is an average across categories, skewed by large advertisers. Pre-product–market-fit startups should be far more activation-heavy.
About 95% of B2B buyers are not in-market right now Ehrenberg-Bass / LinkedIn B2B Institute Category-dependent. The useful implication is durable: most of your audience cannot buy today, so memory beats persuasion.
Rule of 40: growth % + profit margin ≥ 40 SaaS investor heuristic Only meaningful at scale (roughly $10M+ ARR). Early companies routinely and correctly ignore it.
Double jeopardy: small brands have fewer buyers and slightly lower loyalty Byron Sharp, How Brands Grow Rarely breaks, and it is bad news for "we'll win on loyalty" strategies. Penetration usually has to come first.
A 1% conversion-rate lift is usually cheaper than a 1% traffic lift Conversion-optimisation practice Only while you have obvious defects to fix. Once a page is well-optimised, further lifts get expensive fast and traffic wins again.

How to read the numbers on this site

Marketing benchmarks get quoted with far more confidence than they deserve. Published averages mix wildly different business models, they age badly, and the companies that report them are not a random sample.

So everywhere this site shows a range, read it as "the middle of what gets publicly reported" — a sanity check, not a target. If your number sits outside a range here, the first question is whether you measure it the same way the range does, not whether you are failing.

The test that beats any benchmark

Compare this month against your own last three months, on your own definition, with your own data. A trend you measured yourself is worth more than an industry average you didn't.
